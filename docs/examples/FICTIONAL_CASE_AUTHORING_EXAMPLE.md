# FICTIONAL TRAINING EXAMPLE — NOT PARTICIPANT DATA

This document is safe for GitHub because every person and all student information in it are fictional. It is a training artifact only and must not be interpreted as participant data or a real BIP/BSP.

## Fictional Case Profile

- **Case ID:** `CASE-999`
- **Game ID:** `MR-999`
- **Student game alias:** Anna
- **Grade:** Kindergarten
- **Function:** Attention

### Behaviors

Behaviors in this fictional plan may include:

- whining
- tantrums
- not following directions
- hitting
- throwing
- biting

### Functional Pattern

Adult attention is often unavailable immediately, problem behavior occurs, and adult attention frequently follows.

### Replacement Skills

- ask for attention
- ask for help
- deep breathing
- wait appropriately
- follow directions
- calm body/voice

### Plan Components

#### Prevent

- provide non-contingent attention regularly
- provide engaging independent activities
- use visual timers
- give advance reminders

#### Teach

- teach asking for attention/help
- teach breathing
- teach waiting
- practice when calm

#### Reinforce

- provide immediate specific praise
- provide 1:1 attention
- offer adult play
- offer a preferred snack when appropriate

#### Respond

- minimize attention to problem behavior when safe
- prompt replacement behavior
- remain calm and matter-of-fact

### Fidelity Targets

```text
proactive_01      Give non-contingent attention
teaching_01       Prompt replacement behavior
reinforcement_01  Praise
response_01       Ignore
```

General BIP alignment does **NOT** automatically equal exact fidelity linkage. A decision uses a fidelity target key only when that target's exact description is a defensible criterion for scoring the opportunity. For example, a visual timer can align with this fictional BIP without satisfying `proactive_01`.

## Daily Mission Prototype: Everyone Needs You

### Opening Scene

Literacy centers are in full swing. Magnetic letters are scattered across one table, two students are whisper-arguing over a purple marker, and a child beside you is slowly sounding out the word *map*.

Across the room, Anna is building a tall tower with magnetic tiles. She has been playing independently for several minutes, but now she keeps glancing toward you.

Once.

Twice.

Then she holds up a tile and waits.

You know the student beside you needs another minute of help. You also know Anna tends to have a harder time when adult attention disappears for too long.

She hasn't whined, yelled, or left her area yet.

What do you do?

### Decision 1 — The Setup

**Exact fidelity target:** `proactive_01` — Give non-contingent attention

#### 10-point choice

**Teacher response**

> Briefly look over and say, “Anna, I see that huge tower! I’m helping Sam right now, but I’ll check on you again in a few minutes,” then return to the student beside you.

**Modeled consequence**

> Anna’s face lights up when you notice the tower. She looks back at it, adds another tile, and starts arranging pieces along the bottom. The student beside you keeps working through *map*, and for the moment you haven’t had to abandon either child.

**Wizard**

> A tiny spark of attention before the spell goes sideways! Beautiful.

**Explanation**

Giving a small amount of attention before problem behavior occurs directly matches the fictional plan’s prevention target.

**Next:** `SUPPORTED`

#### 5-point choice

**Teacher response**

> Point to the visual timer and say, “When the timer goes off, I can come see what you're building.”

**Modeled consequence**

> Anna watches you point to the timer. She looks at it, then back at you. After a few seconds she returns to the tower—but she keeps checking whether you are looking. Nothing has blown up, but the moment is less settled than it was thirty seconds ago.

**Wizard**

> Useful magic… but one ingredient is missing.

**Explanation**

The timer is plan-aligned, but the exact measured target in this decision is non-contingent attention.

**Next:** `WOBBLY`

#### 0-point choice

**Teacher response**

> Smile and say, “I’m helping someone else right now. You need to keep playing until I’m finished.”

**Modeled consequence**

> Anna stares at you for a moment. Then she lifts the tower a few inches and lets the whole thing crash onto the table. BANG. Three children turn around. “Mrs. Olson! LOOK WHAT HAPPENED!” The student beside you stops sounding out *map* and looks over too.

**Wizard**

> Uh-oh. The attention beacon just got MUCH brighter.

**Explanation**

The response is calm, but it does not use the planned prevention target and the resulting disruption produces a large amount of adult/peer attention.

**Next:** `ESCALATED`

#### Hint

> Nothing has gone wrong yet. That matters. Which choice uses Anna’s plan before she has to work harder to get your attention?

## Mission Design Card

- **Mission ID:** `CASE999_DAILY_01`
- **Mission title:** Everyone Needs You
- **Mission type:** Daily
- **Routine / location:** Literacy centers
- **Central tension:** You must divide attention between Anna and another learner without teaching Anna that disruption is the fastest way to reconnect.
- **Function pressure:** Adult attention becomes scarce while Anna is working independently.
- **Active BIP components:** Prevent, Teach, Reinforce, Respond
- **Exact fidelity opportunities:** D1 `proactive_01`; D2 `teaching_01`; D3 `reinforcement_01`; D4 `response_01`; D5 none
- **Tone:** Busy, playful, recoverable classroom pressure
- **Design goal:** Distinguish preventive attention, an explicit replacement prompt, contingent praise, and low-attention responding from merely kind or immediately effective attention.

The three D1 outcomes above enter the remaining story as `SUPPORTED`, `WOBBLY`, or `ESCALATED`. Each later decision has a scene for every incoming trajectory. Choice metadata is listed as **component · mechanism · error · function**.

## Mission Endings

### `STRONG_FINISH` — The Classroom Reconnects

The bell’s echo fades with Anna participating in the transition rather than competing with it. Depending on the route here, the tower may be standing or packed away, and Sam may have finished every word or only reopened the book. What matters in this ending is the visible comeback: Anna used a functional request, experienced a bounded wait or brief help, and received specific praise and attention for the response the plan seeks to strengthen. Earlier noise still happened on some paths; it did not prevent skillful recovery.

> **Wizard:** “Quest complete! You did not erase the hard moment—you changed what worked next.”

### `MIXED_FINISH` — The Wobble Holds

Anna reaches the next routine without a major new disruption, but she continues checking, hovering, or depending on your proximity. Some useful ingredients appeared—a calm voice, cleanup, a promise, praise, or help—yet their timing or limits were blurry. Your small group lost time, and tomorrow’s attention boundary will need another careful rehearsal.

> **Wizard:** “You crossed the bridge. A few boards are still bouncing, so keep both hands on the map.”

### `FRAGILE_FINISH` — The Shortcut Leaves a Sequel

The room eventually moves on, but the final shortcut creates extra work: the transition is late, Anna remains attached to adult help, or an appropriate request goes unanswered. Anna may look calm in some versions of this ending; the simulation makes clear that calm purchased with immediate extended attention can leave the attention-seeking pattern more efficient next time.

> **Wizard:** “The scene ended, not the story. Next quest: make the skill faster than the shortcut.”
