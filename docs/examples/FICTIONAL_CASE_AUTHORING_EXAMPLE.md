# FICTIONAL TRAINING EXAMPLE — NOT PARTICIPANT DATA

This document is safe for GitHub because every person and all student information in it are fictional. It is a training artifact only and must not be interpreted as participant data, a real BIP/BSP, or a complete mission.

## Fictional Case Profile

- **Case ID:** `CASE-999`
- **Game ID:** `MR-999`
- **Student game alias:** Anna
- **Grade:** Kindergarten
- **Function:** Attention

### Behaviors

Behaviors in this fictional plan may include:

- whining
- tantrums
- not following directions
- hitting
- throwing
- biting

### Functional Pattern

Adult attention is often unavailable immediately, problem behavior occurs, and adult attention frequently follows.

### Replacement Skills

- ask for attention
- ask for help
- deep breathing
- wait appropriately
- follow directions
- calm body/voice

### Plan Components

#### Prevent

- provide non-contingent attention regularly
- provide engaging independent activities
- use visual timers
- give advance reminders

#### Teach

- teach asking for attention/help
- teach breathing
- teach waiting
- practice when calm

#### Reinforce

- provide immediate specific praise
- provide 1:1 attention
- offer adult play
- offer a preferred snack when appropriate

#### Respond

- minimize attention to problem behavior when safe
- prompt replacement behavior
- remain calm and matter-of-fact

### Fidelity Targets

```text
proactive_01      Give non-contingent attention
teaching_01       Prompt replacement behavior
reinforcement_01  Praise
response_01       Ignore
```

General BIP alignment does **NOT** automatically equal exact fidelity linkage. A decision uses a fidelity target key only when that target's exact description is a defensible criterion for scoring the opportunity. For example, a visual timer can align with this fictional BIP without satisfying `proactive_01`.

## Daily Mission Prototype: Everyone Needs You

### Opening Scene

Literacy centers are in full swing. Magnetic letters are scattered across one table, two students are whisper-arguing over a purple marker, and a child beside you is slowly sounding out the word *map*.

Across the room, Anna is building a tall tower with magnetic tiles. She has been playing independently for several minutes, but now she keeps glancing toward you.

Once.

Twice.

Then she holds up a tile and waits.

You know the student beside you needs another minute of help. You also know Anna tends to have a harder time when adult attention disappears for too long.

She hasn't whined, yelled, or left her area yet.

What do you do?

### Decision 1 — The Setup

**Exact fidelity target:** `proactive_01` — Give non-contingent attention

#### 10-point choice

**Teacher response**

> Briefly look over and say, “Anna, I see that huge tower! I’m helping Sam right now, but I’ll check on you again in a few minutes,” then return to the student beside you.

**Modeled consequence**

> Anna’s face lights up when you notice the tower. She looks back at it, adds another tile, and starts arranging pieces along the bottom. The student beside you keeps working through *map*, and for the moment you haven’t had to abandon either child.

**Wizard**

> A tiny spark of attention before the spell goes sideways! Beautiful.

**Explanation**

Giving a small amount of attention before problem behavior occurs directly matches the fictional plan’s prevention target.

**Next:** `SUPPORTED`

#### 5-point choice

**Teacher response**

> Point to the visual timer and say, “When the timer goes off, I can come see what you're building.”

**Modeled consequence**

> Anna watches you point to the timer. She looks at it, then back at you. After a few seconds she returns to the tower—but she keeps checking whether you are looking. Nothing has blown up, but the moment is less settled than it was thirty seconds ago.

**Wizard**

> Useful magic… but one ingredient is missing.

**Explanation**

The timer is plan-aligned, but the exact measured target in this decision is non-contingent attention.

**Next:** `WOBBLY`

#### 0-point choice

**Teacher response**

> Smile and say, “I’m helping someone else right now. You need to keep playing until I’m finished.”

**Modeled consequence**

> Anna stares at you for a moment. Then she lifts the tower a few inches and lets the whole thing crash onto the table. BANG. Three children turn around. “Mrs. Olson! LOOK WHAT HAPPENED!” The student beside you stops sounding out *map* and looks over too.

**Wizard**

> Uh-oh. The attention beacon just got MUCH brighter.

**Explanation**

The response is calm, but it does not use the planned prevention target and the resulting disruption produces a large amount of adult/peer attention.

**Next:** `ESCALATED`

#### Hint

> Nothing has gone wrong yet. That matters. Which choice uses Anna’s plan before she has to work harder to get your attention?

## Why This Example Works

- All choices sound plausible to a competent educator.
- The 5-point choice is genuinely plan-aligned but incomplete for the exact opportunity.
- The 0-point choice is realistic, not cruel or absurd.
- Each consequence is visible in the classroom.
- Each choice creates a different next state.
- The attention function becomes apparent through gameplay rather than vocabulary testing.
- Fidelity linkage is exact: Decision 1 directly measures non-contingent attention.
- The feedback explains the behavioral mechanism without judging the teacher.
- The scenario feels like a classroom rather than a quiz.

Decisions 2–5 are intentionally not included. The purpose of this fictional example is to freeze the prototype standard before expanding the mission.
